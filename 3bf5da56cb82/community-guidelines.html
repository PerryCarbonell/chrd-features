CHRD Community Guidelines

Last updated: August 2026

Welcome

CHRD is for music, live culture, and the people around it. These guidelines explain how we keep the community useful and safe. They work alongside our Terms of Service and Privacy Policy.

Be 18+, verify your age when asked, and treat others the way you’d want to be treated at a show.

Do

  • Share music, events, and culture authentically
  • Support local artists, venues, and scenes
  • Post only music and media you’re allowed to share — your own work, licensed, or clearly permitted
  • Flag content that feels off, spammy, unsafe, or out of place
  • Engage respectfully with others’ posts, listens, and events
  • Give credit when you share someone else’s work
  • Use Report / block tools when someone crosses a line

Don’t

  • Post off-topic spam, scams, or content that isn’t about music / culture / events
  • Harass, hate, threaten, dox, or bully anyone
  • Share others’ precise locations, private addresses, or personal info without consent
  • Impersonate people, venues, or brands
  • Post illegal content, or content that exploits or endangers minors
  • Upload full tracks, albums, or artwork you don’t own or aren’t allowed to share (see Music & copyright)
  • Use CHRD for commercial promotion unrelated to music or live culture without permission
  • Bypass blocks, age checks, or safety tools

What happens when you flag content

Anyone can report posts or events. Reports help us (and automatic checks) keep feeds clean.

  • Off-topic: about 3 unique off-topic reports → content is hidden from public feeds (Home, Search, Signal discovery). It may still appear on the creator’s profile or as a limited listing so they stay accountable.
  • Suspicious / harmful: about 2 unique suspicious reports → content is hidden from feeds pending review
  • We review flagged items and may remove content, limit features, or suspend accounts that break these guidelines or the Terms
  • False or retaliatory mass-reporting can itself lead to action
  • Community Report flags are not the same as copyright strikes (those are explained under Music & copyright and in the Terms)

Music & copyright

You’re responsible for what you post. Only share music, video, and images you own, have licensed, or are clearly allowed to share. Credit is good manners — it doesn’t replace permission.

We don’t scan every upload before it goes live. If a rights holder sends a valid copyright notice (or we reasonably believe we must act), we may remove or disable the content and log a copyright strike against the account. The formal ladder lives in the Terms of Service; this section explains what that means in practice.

What usually counts toward a strike

  • A label or rights holder emails a clear notice about your post of a full album or track rip → we remove it → typically 1 strike
  • The same account later gets another upheld notice on different content → another strike
  • Around three strikes: we may warn you, limit posting or other features, or take other action that seems fair
  • Around five strikes: we may suspend or terminate the account
  • Dumping lots of commercial tracks in a short time can skip the slow ladder — serious or commercial-scale infringement can lead to faster action

What usually does not mean an automatic strike

  • Someone hitting Report in the app — that’s a community flag, not a copyright strike by itself
  • You filming yourself at a gig, dancing in a café, or sharing a short ambient clip — often a grey area; we usually act when a rights holder complains, not on vibes alone
  • Your own song, a cover you’re allowed to post, label-approved promo, or music you’ve licensed
  • A notice you successfully dispute and we put the content back — that shouldn’t stick as a lasting strike
  • “Other apps let me” — other platforms have different tools and deals; on CHRD you’re still responsible for your uploads

If you believe your work was posted without permission

Email contactpcarbonell@gmail.com with your contact details, what was copied, and where it appears in the app. See Terms of Service → Copyright complaints for the full notice process.

What CHRD looks like (not exhaustive)

Music discovery and sharing

  • Artist promoting a new release (“Album drops Friday — first single’s up”)
  • Fan sharing what they’re spinning (“Rare 70s jazz this morning”)
  • Record shop posting new arrivals (“Just in: first pressing…”)

Events and live culture

  • Venue announcing a listening party or club night
  • Festival or promoter sharing a lineup
  • Band promoting an upcoming gig
  • Skate shop hosting music (“Skate + Sounds — local DJ, open session”)

Community moments

  • Podcast creator sharing a live recording
  • DJ previewing an unreleased track
  • Curator launching a themed playlist
  • Collector sharing a rare find
  • Musician posting studio / behind-the-scenes moments
  • Signal listens and nearby matches when you’re both on the same track

Questions

Safety or guideline questions: contactpcarbonell@gmail.com

Serious harm or illegal activity may also be reported to authorities.
